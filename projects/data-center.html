Mapping Data Center Development Suitability in Michigan

Where can Michigan accommodate data center development while accounting for infrastructure readiness, environmental constraints, and local policy conditions?

Conceptual project illustration; not a completed research output.

Why this question matters

Data center siting brings together grid capacity, land, fiber connectivity, water, environmental risks, and local governance. A location that looks technically suitable may still face meaningful environmental or institutional constraints.

Geographic scope and research questions

This ongoing study examines Michigan statewide, using its 83 counties as primary comparison units and selected municipalities for closer examination of zoning and local policy.

Which areas appear more suitable under different criteria and priorities? How do rankings change when energy infrastructure, environmental sustainability, and policy considerations receive different weights? Where do physical suitability and local policy readiness diverge?

GIS-MCDA methodology

Energy infrastructureElectricity and fiberWater and flood riskEnergy sustainabilityZoning and incentives

The proposed workflow combines spatial data collection, GIS proximity and overlay analysis, criterion normalization, and weighted linear combination. An equal-weight baseline will be compared with energy- and environment-priority scenarios, with sensitivity analysis and comparison to known data center locations.

Expected outputs

Planned outputs include a Michigan data center inventory, individual energy and environmental criterion maps, composite suitability maps, scenario comparisons, and an assessment of physical versus local-policy suitability. These are expected outputs, not completed findings.

Policy and local governance component

Beyond physical suitability, this research examines how local zoning, permitting, environmental safeguards, and potential incentives influence the feasibility and desirability of data center development. The aim is to distinguish technically promising locations from locations that may face policy or community constraints.

County-level GIS comparisons will be complemented by a closer review of selected local-government approaches. This policy assessment is part of the ongoing research design; it does not yet establish that any specific county or municipality is suitable.
