Industrial Decarbonization in Wayne County

Exploring practical pathways to reduce industrial emissions through facility-level analysis, technology assessment, and economic and policy evaluation.

Conceptual project illustration; research is ongoing.

Project overview

This project works with Wayne County’s Department of Sustainability & Innovation through the University of Michigan Dow Sustainability Fellows Program, administered by the Graham Sustainability Institute. The focus is on identifying realistic industrial decarbonization opportunities that account for technical feasibility, economics, and policy.

Research questions

Which industrial facilities and emission sources represent potential decarbonization opportunities? What technologies and infrastructure would be required? How do investment costs, available incentives, and policy design affect the feasibility of different pathways?

Research approach

The work includes developing a facility-level emissions database covering 25 major industrial emitters, using SQL, Python, and GIS to organize and analyze data. It also examines technology readiness, costs, policy incentives, and economic modeling of potential cap-and-invest scenarios.

Policy and economic component

The analysis considers how financing, incentives, and possible carbon-policy mechanisms could influence industrial investment decisions. The goal is to connect technical decarbonization options with implementable policy and economic pathways.

Status and expected outputs

Research is ongoing. Anticipated outputs include structured emissions analysis, assessment of decarbonization options, and economic and policy insights. Partner-approved findings and materials will be shared when available.

Program affiliation

Wayne County is the community project partner; Dow Sustainability Fellows and the Graham Sustainability Institute support the fellowship program.
