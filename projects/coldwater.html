2025 / Renewable energy / Municipal infrastructure

Coldwater
Solar Carport
Feasibility Study.

Evaluated the feasibility of solar carports at municipal sites in Coldwater, Michigan, combining system design, energy modeling, economic considerations, and implementation planning.

SketchUp · NREL SAM · PVsyst · Coldwater, Michigan

01 / The challenge

Can a parking lot become
clean energy infrastructure?

Coldwater, Michigan faces a familiar question for northern communities: can solar energy make sense despite snowy winters and seasonal changes in sunlight?

Solar carports offer a dual-use approach, generating electricity above existing parking spaces without requiring a separate ground-mounted solar site. They can also provide shade and some shelter for parked vehicles.

The challenge is finding designs that work for the city's parking needs, weather, infrastructure, and budget.

02 / Solar resource potential

What about
Michigan winters?

Before designing a system, I examined whether Coldwater's seasonal solar resource could support meaningful electricity generation.

The project's solar resource assessment showed approximately 2.7–6.2 kWh/m²/day across the year, with stronger solar radiation from May through August. Lower winter sunlight remains an important design and modeling consideration, but does not eliminate the potential for solar generation.

03 / Design thinking & assessment

A framework for
practical decisions.

Developed a planning framework to assess community fit, site selection, foundations and weather conditions, solar resources, parking layout, energy generation, potential EV charging and storage integration, project costs, financing, and environmental impacts.

04 / Pilot design & energy modeling

From concept to
modeled performance.

Designed and compared two carport configurations in SketchUp, then assessed photovoltaic performance using NREL SAM and PVsyst.

The modeled alternatives helped evaluate how system design and site constraints affect electricity production.

05 / Site scalability

Looking beyond
one parking lot.

Assessed five locations for potential solar carport expansion, considering site layout, available parking areas, and opportunities for integrating solar infrastructure into municipal planning.

06 / Technical & economic findings

What could the
investment deliver?

The pilot estimate for the Henry L. Brown Municipal Building was 119.6 kW of installed DC capacity and approximately 164,045 kWh of annual electricity generation — equivalent to roughly 38% of the building's annual electricity use.

The project presentation also estimated $13,500–$13,800 per year in electricity value for the pilot. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed saving or a full financial return.

Across all five assessed sites, the combined scenario totaled approximately 1,171.6 kW of capacity and 1,743,045 kWh in annual modeled output.

These figures illustrate the potential scale of a phased approach, subject to site-specific engineering, costs, financing, and implementation decisions.
